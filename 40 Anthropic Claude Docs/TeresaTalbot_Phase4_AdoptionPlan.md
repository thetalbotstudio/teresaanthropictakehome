Ensuring adoption is where I shine. I've implemented tools, processes, and guideline at Salesforce with great success. For this project, I’d use a staged adoption plan. First, I’d start with the eager product teams. I'd provide a migration kit with workflows, templates, metadata rules, redirect helpers, and worked examples. Second, I'd track everything: tasks to complete and who's responsible, before and after pages, and success metrics for finished pages. I make adjustments where needed before implementing company wide.

Next, I’d evangelize results with the remaining teams. I’d advertise the before-and-after results from the first group, then add the workflows to the normal publication path. A dashboard would track ownership, compliance, and freshness by team. It's good to establish a lead for each product line, while the central docs group manages taxonomy, redirects, and shared definitions.

For teams that are slow to adopt the system, I’d start with communication. In my experience, product teams want good product documentation. Finding the blocker and removing it is paramount. Hopefully, it's a little more handholding or education in the benefits of the new procedures. If there's a deeper conflict, a disagreement with the process in general, a conference with a higher decision maker would be the fastest way to a solution. My priority is when the gap creates security, access, launch, or customer-support risk.
